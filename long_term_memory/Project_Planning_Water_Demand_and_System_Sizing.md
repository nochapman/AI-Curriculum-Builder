# Project Planning: Calculating Water Demand and System Sizing

## Objective
Users will be able to calculate their daily water needs (16 gallons for 4 people over 4 days), determine required storage volume, and estimate catchment area based on local rainfall data to meet their potable water goal.

## Daily Potable Water Needs

While general recommendations for total daily water intake (including from food and other beverages) vary, often suggesting 2.7 liters (11.5 cups) for women and 3.7 liters (15.5 cups) for men, the specific potable water goal for this objective is **1 gallon per person per day**. This translates to **16 gallons for 4 people over 4 days** (16 gallons / 4 people / 4 days = 1 gallon/person/day). Factors such as activity level, climate, health, and body size can influence individual water requirements.

## Required Storage Volume

To determine the necessary water storage volume, you must first establish the daily water demand and the number of days for which water needs to be stored (e.g., to cover dry periods). Using the objective's potable water goal:

*   **Daily demand for 4 people:** 4 people * 1 gallon/person/day = 4 gallons/day
*   **Storage for 4 days:** 4 gallons/day * 4 days = 16 gallons

For calculating the physical dimensions of a storage tank, common formulas are used:

*   **Rectangular Tank:** Volume (in cubic feet) = Length × Width × Height. To convert cubic feet to gallons, multiply by 7.48 gallons/cubic foot.
*   **Cylindrical Tank:** Volume (in cubic feet) = π × (Radius)² × Height. Then, multiply by 7.48 gallons/cubic foot to get gallons.

## Catchment Area Estimation

Estimating the catchment area involves calculating the surface area that will collect rainwater and then determining the potential volume of water that can be harvested based on local rainfall data. The most common catchment surface is a roof.

The formula for calculating potential rainwater harvest is:

**Rainwater Harvest (in Gallons) = Catchment Area (in Square Feet) × Volume of Rainfall (in Inches) × 0.623**.

*   **Catchment Area:** For square or rectangular roofs, this is simply the length multiplied by the width of the roof's "footprint," without needing to account for the roof's slope.
*   **Local Rainfall Data:** Accurate local rainfall data (e.g., average annual or monthly rainfall) is essential for these calculations.
*   **Efficiency:** It's important to consider system efficiency, as factors like evaporation, roof material, and pipe sizing can affect the actual amount of water collected. Well-designed systems can capture 75-85% of total rainfall. Additionally, a "first flush" diverter is recommended to route the initial, dirtier runoff away from the storage tank to maintain water quality.
